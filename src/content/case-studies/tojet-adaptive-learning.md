## Overview

TOJET’s first lesson format was inspired by Duolingo’s fast, interactive learning model: short multiple-choice questions, instant feedback, and repeated practice until mastery.

It was simple, scalable, and engaging—but as TOJET expanded into more subjects, one problem became clear: a single learning format couldn’t support very different learning needs.

![TOJET adaptive learning overview](/case-studies/tojet-adaptive-learning/overview.png "wide")

## The Problem

Our original quiz-based flow worked well for memory-heavy subjects like History, Social Studies, and vocabulary.

But it broke down for concept-heavy subjects. Math needed step-by-step reasoning, Arabic needed explanations and examples, and problem-solving topics needed teaching before testing.

Students were being asked to answer before they were ready.

This challenge also aligned with [Duolingo’s learning approach](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-experts-work-with-ai/), where learning objectives are carefully sequenced, concepts build gradually, and AI helps personalize exercises around each learner’s needs.

## Product Goal

Design a learning system that adapts to the subject—not the other way around.

## Quiz-Based Lessons

We kept the original quiz-based format where it worked best: facts, definitions, recall practice, and quick revision.

Its strength was speed—students could move through short learning loops, get immediate feedback, and reinforce what they already knew without unnecessary explanation.

![TOJET quiz-based learning format](/case-studies/tojet-adaptive-learning/quiz-based-lessons.png "wide")

## AI-Guided Conversational Lessons

For Math, Arabic, and other explanation-heavy subjects, we introduced an AI-guided conversational lesson format.

Instead of testing students immediately, the AI tutor walked them through short explanations, examples, and progressive steps. Students could ask for more detail, request another example, or continue when they felt ready.

Practice happened within the same flow: correct answers moved students forward, while mistakes triggered a new explanation before retrying. Each lesson ended with a short mastery quiz, which also fed into TOJET’s spaced-review system.

This direction was influenced by [Khan Academy’s Khanmigo approach](https://blog.khanacademy.org/harnessing-ai-so-that-all-students-benefit-a-nonprofit-approach-for-equal-access/), where AI supports learning through back-and-forth questioning and guided tutoring rather than simply giving students the answer.

![TOJET AI-guided conversational learning](/case-studies/tojet-adaptive-learning/ai-guided-lessons.png "wide")

## Flashcard Lessons

For Arabic and English vocabulary, short concepts, and quick review sessions, we introduced lightweight flashcard lessons.

Different visual styles kept repeated practice from feeling monotonous, while the format made content easier to scan, recall, and revisit.

This approach also reflected patterns seen in tools like Quizlet, where lightweight repetition can make quick review more effective.

By structuring lessons into roughly 10–15 minute sessions, students could move through content faster and with less effort than traditional study patterns.

![TOJET flashcard learning format](/case-studies/tojet-adaptive-learning/flashcard-lessons.png "wide")

## A Valuable User Insight

Students shared an unexpected piece of feedback:

> “The multiple-choice lessons feel like taking a test.”

That mattered. Even an effective learning format can create friction if it feels stressful or overly evaluative.

We realized educational UX isn’t only about efficiency—it also needs to support emotional comfort.

This became an important direction for future iterations.

## My Role

I led the product and UX direction for the learning experience—identifying friction, redesigning lesson flows, and shaping a scalable system that could support different subjects and learning needs.

## Impact

TOJET evolved from a single lesson mechanic into a more adaptive learning platform.

Instead of asking:

> “How do we make one format work for everything?”

We started asking:

> “What is the best way to teach this topic?”

That shift changed how we approached every learning feature that followed.

## Outcomes

The redesigned learning system expanded TOJET from one lesson format to four, making it possible to support a wider range of subjects.

By generating multiple lesson outputs from a single input, the system increased content efficiency by around 2–3x.

By combining active recall with reinforcement quizzes, the system also showed a 25–30% improvement in retention strength.

![TOJET adaptive learning outcomes](/case-studies/tojet-adaptive-learning/outcomes.png "wide")

## Future Direction

We also began exploring learning formats beyond text-based lessons, including short audio lessons, AI-generated video explanations, and gamified exercises such as fill-in-the-blank and matching.

The direction stayed the same: match each learning moment with the format that supports it best, rather than forcing everything into one fixed system.

![TOJET future learning formats](/case-studies/tojet-adaptive-learning/future-learning-formats.png "wide")

## What I Learned

Students don’t always struggle because learning is hard.

Sometimes they struggle because the learning format is wrong.
