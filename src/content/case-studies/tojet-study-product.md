## Overview

TOJET was designed for high-school humanities students in Iran, where exam preparation often meant expensive tutoring, fragmented content, or rigid study materials.

The real problem wasn’t access to content, but consistency and structure. We wanted to make studying feel more guided, personal, and less overwhelming without making it expensive.

![TOJET study product overview](/case-studies/tojet-study-product/overview.png "wide")

## My Role

I worked as the product designer across the full product cycle—defining features, shaping flows, prioritizing what mattered, and iterating from real user feedback.

Much of the product direction evolved through building and testing rather than being fixed upfront.

## What We Kept Seeing

A few patterns emerged early: students weren’t struggling with motivation, but with knowing where to start, staying consistent, and using tools that matched how they actually learned.

We also found that different subjects created very different kinds of friction, which meant one learning approach wouldn’t work for everything.

## Planning Was the Real Product

Personalized study planning became one of TOJET’s most-used features.

Students set their available study time, plan duration, goal, grade level, and optional subject exclusions, and the system generated a structured plan around them. What looked like a simple feature removed a major source of decision fatigue.

![TOJET personalized study planning](/case-studies/tojet-study-product/study-planning.png "wide")

## Motivation Wasn’t Enough

We initially assumed streaks, points, and milestones would drive motivation.

They helped, but clear visibility of progress mattered more. So instead of over-gamifying the experience, we focused on making progress easy to see and understand.

![TOJET league and progress visibility](/case-studies/tojet-study-product/league-progress.png "wide")

## Learning System: One Format Didn’t Survive Reality

We started with a quiz-based learning flow, which worked well for memorization-heavy subjects but broke down for subjects like Arabic and Math, where students needed explanation before practice.

So we introduced AI-guided explanations that worked more like a tutor—explaining concepts, answering questions, then moving into exercises. We also used flashcards for Arabic and English vocabulary, plus short key points for quick review sessions.

The goal was simple: stop forcing one learning format across every subject.

![TOJET adaptive learning system](/case-studies/tojet-study-product/learning-system.png "wide")

## Performance Feedback

We added a lightweight performance layer showing completed quizzes, study time, streak consistency, and points earned.

The goal wasn’t complex analytics, just enough feedback to make progress feel visible and real. We also experimented with simple insights around students’ strengths and weaker areas.

![TOJET performance feedback and study statistics](/case-studies/tojet-study-product/performance-feedback.png "wide")

## Community

We added a lightweight social layer where students could share achievements and progress, but intentionally avoided chat and open messaging.

The goal was motivation through visibility, not distraction—a decision shaped by early feedback showing that focus mattered more than interaction.

![TOJET community and social progress](/case-studies/tojet-study-product/community.png "wide")

## Constraints Shaped Most Decisions

TOJET was built under real constraints: limited resources, fast iteration cycles, broad user needs, and a constant trade-off between engagement and simplicity.

Many of the product decisions came down not only to what we built, but what we deliberately chose not to build.

![TOJET learning characters](/case-studies/tojet-study-product/tojet-characters.png "wide")


## Outcome

TOJET gained early traction, but the stronger signal came from how students actually used the product.

![3,500+ TOJET installs](/case-studies/tojet-study-product/outcome-installs.png "half")
![100+ TOJET daily active users](/case-studies/tojet-study-product/outcome-daily-users.png "half")

More important than the numbers was the pattern behind them: students consistently engaged more with planning and structured learning than with isolated content, and affordability proved to matter in actual usage—not just as a promise.

TOJET reinforced a simple idea: students rarely struggle because content is missing; they struggle when momentum breaks. If I rebuilt it today, I’d push further into adaptive planning and deeper personalization while keeping the same principle: great education products don’t just teach—they help people keep going.
